\section{Adapting the machinery to $x_c^{(6)}=-\Lambda$}
$x_c^{(6)}=-\Lambda$ is real and negative; $\theta^*=0$ exactly at $N=6$, so the natural region to certify
is the \textbf{real segment} $\operatorname{Re}x\in[X_0,\Lambda]$ ($\operatorname{Im}x=0$), which is
exactly the $\operatorname{Re}x>0$ portion of the legs starting at $x_c^{(6)}$ before they turn onto the
constant-height lines $\operatorname{Im}x=\mp\pi/6$ (room20 \S4). The region $\operatorname{Re}x<0$
(between $x_c^{(6)}$ and this segment) is the crossing region room20 \S5 left open (Poisson-summation
bookkeeping, not attempted here either) -- this room does not touch it.

\textbf{First attempt (failed, informative):} directly reuse P1i's ``connect $x_A$ to the saddle $x_\mu$ by
a straight segment, then bound by the centred form'' machinery, with $x_A$ ranging over $[X_0,\Lambda]$
in place of $[\delta/2,2\delta]$. This fails immediately for every $\Lambda\gtrsim1$: the segment from the
(small, near-origin) saddle location out to a box at distance $\Lambda$ has box radius $O(\Lambda)$, and
the centred-form error term $\operatorname{rad}(X)|\Omega_\mu'(X)|$ grows like $\Lambda^2$, swamping the
bound (checked: at $\Lambda=8$, $X_0=0.05$, sampled maximum $\approx14.8$, nowhere near
$-\eta=-0.001$). P1i's own machinery is built for a box adjacent to the saddle; it is the wrong tool for
a long real-axis run and was not force-fit.

\textbf{Working approach} (\texttt{room22\_landscape\_cert.py}): bound $\operatorname{Wup}$ (P1i's Arb
centred-form box bound for $\operatorname{Re}((\Omega_\mu(x)-V_{\mathrm{ref}})e^{-i\theta})$, reused
verbatim from \texttt{P1i\_landscape\_cert.py}) directly on adaptively-bisected sub-boxes of the real
interval $[X_0,\Lambda]$ itself, for every explicit mode $\mu$ of P1f's $N=6$ mode decomposition (reused
verbatim: 22 modes for $B$, 22 for $S_\pm$, plus the two remainder modes each) -- no connecting segment
to the saddle. $\theta$ is certified $\approx0$ (the box $\Theta=\theta^*\pm10^{-5}$, so
$\cos\Theta>0.99999$ is checked explicitly, giving condition (d), $\operatorname{Re}x>0$, immediately
since $X_0>0$). For a mode whose saddle real part $\operatorname{Re}x_\mu$ lies inside $[X_0,\Lambda]$,
the interval is split to exclude a window $[\operatorname{Re}x_\mu-w,\operatorname{Re}x_\mu+w]$ around it
(at its own saddle the height touches its own reference by definition, so no negative margin can hold
there -- this is the region P1i's own delta-box, Sec.\ref{sec:existing}, already covers, at $N=6$ only
for $S_\pm$). Every surviving piece is required to certify strictly below $-\eta=-10^{-3}$ (the same
$\eta$ as P1i), via the same adaptive bisection / sampled-target scheme as \texttt{P1i\_landscape\_cert.py}
(\texttt{scan}, \texttt{target}, imported unmodified). Remainder-mode rays start at $\operatorname{Re}x=\Lambda$
and reuse P1f's part-(iii) analytic tail bound (also imported unmodified).

\section{A genuine finding: $B$'s dominant modes leave a ridge near $\operatorname{Re}x\approx0.35$--$0.88$}
Direct floating(-then-certified) evaluation of $\Omega_{-3}(x)-V_{-3}$ on the real axis for $B$ (dominant
modes $\mu=-3,-1$, whose saddles sit at $\operatorname{Re}x=0.349119\ldots\mp i\pi/6$, i.e.\ \emph{off}
the real axis by $\pi/6$) shows the height is \textbf{positive} on the real axis near the saddles' real
part, not just in a thin window:
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccccccc}
x & 0.3 & 0.5 & 0.7 & 0.8 & 0.85 & 0.87 & 0.9\\\hline
\operatorname{Re}(\Omega_{-3}(x)-V_{-3}) & +0.272 & +0.251 & +0.150 & +0.069 & +0.021 & +0.001 & -0.032
\end{array}
\]
so the sign change is at $x^\dagger\approx0.879$ (window search: $X_0=0.87$ certificate FAILS at margin
$+0.00099$, $X_0=0.88$ CERTIFIES at margin $-0.005$). This is a genuine geometric fact about the $N=6$
landscape, not a bug: the two off-axis dominant saddles leave a residual positive ridge on the real axis
(the tie ray itself) out to $\operatorname{Re}x\approx0.88$, well past their own real part $0.349$. A
window of $w=0.05$ around the saddle (as tried first) is far too small; the certificate below uses
$X_0\ge0.88>x^\dagger$, i.e.\ it starts the certified real segment strictly past this ridge.

\section*{Verdict}
\textbf{CERTIFICATE PASSES}, rigorously (Arb, 128-bit, python-flint), for the real segment
$\operatorname{Re}x\in[X_0,\Lambda]$, $\operatorname{Im}x=0$ (the exact tie-ray direction $\theta^*=0$ of
$N=6$), for both $X_0=1,\Lambda=10$ and $X_0=1,\Lambda=50$ (both $B$ and $S_\pm$), and at the sharp
boundary $X_0=0.88$ (just past the ridge of \S3). Margins: for $B$ at $[1,10]$, non-dominant
$\le-0.0779$, dominant-far $\le-0.1155$, remainder $\le-85.8$; for $S_\pm$, non-dominant $\le-0.2153$,
dominant-far $\le-0.3672$, remainder $\le-85.7$ (all Arb upper bounds, all comfortably below $-\eta=-10^{-3}$).

\textbf{What this establishes.} It is a genuine, real, rigorous piece: it certifies that the landscape
bound needed for room20's contour construction (\S4 there: ``the ray-lemma-based error term ... is under
control on all of $\Gamma_-^{(6)},\Pi_0^{(6)},\Gamma_+^{(6)}$ with $\operatorname{Re}x>0$'') is not merely
qualitatively plausible but Arb-certified, concretely, on the real-axis approach to those legs out to
$\operatorname{Re}x=50$, for both $B$ and $S_\pm$. It also finds and locates, rigorously, a real
obstruction not previously flagged anywhere in Rooms 1--21: \textbf{$B$'s landscape on the real axis is
NOT uniformly healthy for $\operatorname{Re}x>0$ -- it fails for $\operatorname{Re}x\lesssim0.879$},
because the two off-axis dominant saddles ($\mu=-3,-1$ at $\operatorname{Re}x=0.349\mp i\pi/6$) leave a
positive ridge on the real axis reaching almost to $\operatorname{Re}x=0.88$, well past their own real
part. This narrows (but does not close) where a future crossing-region argument (room20 \S5, still open)
must land: any contour construction starting at $x_c^{(6)}=-\Lambda$ and using this real-axis approach
must clear $\operatorname{Re}x\gtrsim0.88$ before the $B$-landscape bound of this room applies; between
$x_c^{(6)}$ (very negative) and $\operatorname{Re}x\approx0.88$, neither this room nor room20 \S5
certifies anything.

\textbf{What this does NOT establish} (be honest, per task instructions):
\begin{itemize}
\item It does not certify $x_c^{(6)}$ itself, which has $\operatorname{Re}x<0$: at $x_c^{(6)}$,
$\Omega_\mu$'s holomorphy/boundedness is exactly the open crossing-region question of room20 \S5
(Poisson summation over 6 residue classes), untouched here.
\item It does not certify the constant-height legs $\operatorname{Im}x=\mp\pi/6$ themselves (only the
real-axis, $\operatorname{Im}x=0$ approach segment); those are a separate geometric region, covered (per
room20 \S4, not re-verified here) by Rooms 17/19's separation-constant bound.
\item It is not a certificate of the Rouch\'e step, which room20's own verdict already flags as blocked on
the crossing-region constants (\S5 there) regardless of this room's result.
\item It does not resolve $B$'s head failure (Sec.\ref{sec:existing}); that is a separate, already-known
obstruction (room20 \S1--2), orthogonal to the landscape question this room answers.
\item The window-based mode-exclusion trick (\S2--3) is legitimate but ad hoc: it was tuned reactively
(first $w=0.05$ failed, discovered the ridge, then set $X_0$ past it) rather than derived analytically;
a fully general statement (for arbitrary $X_0$ near the ridge, or for other $N$) would need the ridge's
exact location derived in closed form, not just bisected numerically-then-certified at the two tested
points.
\end{itemize}
