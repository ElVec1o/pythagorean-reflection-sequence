\section*{Room of mathematicians, item 8 (2026-09-26): closing Erd\H os's derivative-bound gap}

\paragraph{Calibration point.} Reproduced (\texttt{room1\_seat\_erdos\_rouche.py}, unchanged): for
$j=4$ ($t_0\approx0.02732181489+0.001017999912i$, near $\zeta=e(1/6)$), the discrete winding number
of $\arg B$ around the disk $|t-t_0|=\rho\approx0.12|t_0|\approx0.00328$, sampled at 24 points with
60-digit Arb ball arithmetic, is exactly $1$. This matches Room~1's report verbatim and confirms the
starting point before attempting to close the gap Reviewer~AQ flagged: no explicit
modulus-of-continuity/derivative bound on $B$ had been derived, so the winding-number evidence, however
stress-tested (up to $400$ samples, $16\times$ density, no instability), was still empirical rather than
a rigorous exclusion of aliasing between samples.

\paragraph{What was attempted and rejected first.} A hand-derived closed-form tail bound on
$B'$, built the same way as the $B$-tail bound is described in the note (bound $|1-q^j|\ge1-|q|^j$
term-by-term and multiply out), was tried first and \textbf{failed}: at $N=6$'s tie point $q$ has
modulus $|q|\approx0.97$--$0.98$ but argument $\approx\pi/3$ (not near the positive real axis), so the
crude bound $|1-q^j|\ge1-|q|^j$ -- which only uses $|q|$, not $\arg q$ -- is many orders of magnitude too
pessimistic (it treats every $j$ as if $q^j$ were real and close to $1$). This blew the bound up to
$\sim\!10^{26}$--$10^{47}$, useless. A second attempt using Arb interval (ball) arithmetic directly on a
\emph{square enclosure} of the whole disk in $q$-space also failed: propagating an interval through
$q^{k^2}$ for $k$ up to $60$--$70$ causes catastrophic overestimation (the ball radius explodes to
\texttt{nan}) well before the terms have decayed -- interval exponentiation to a large power is not a
safe way to get a tail bound here. Both dead ends are recorded so they are not retried.

\paragraph{What worked: a Cauchy-estimate derivative bound from a rigorous sup-bound on $B$ itself.}
$B(q)=\sum_{k\ge0}(-2(1-q))^kq^{k^2}/(q;q)_{2k}$ has $(q;q)_{2k}=\prod_{j=1}^{2k}(1-q^j)\ne0$
whenever $|q|<1$ (never a root of unity), so $B$ is holomorphic with no singularities on any disk
$|t-t_0|\le R$ small enough that $|q(t)|<1$ throughout -- true here for $R$ several times $\rho$, since
$|q(t)|=e^{-\operatorname{Re}t}$ stays $\le0.98$. This lets Cauchy's estimate do the work instead of
differentiating the series term-by-term:
\[
\sup_{|t-t_0|\le\rho}|B'(t)|\;\le\;\frac{R}{(R-\rho)^2}\sup_{|z-t_0|=R}|B(z)|,\qquad R=2\rho.
\]
Both the boundary values $B(t)$ (on $|t-t_0|=\rho$) and the larger-circle values $B(z)$ (on
$|z-t_0|=2\rho$) are computed rigorously, at exact sample points (not intervals), with a
\emph{validated} geometric tail bound: at each sample point the ratio $|a_{k+1}|/|a_k|$ of consecutive
series terms is computed exactly (Arb, 300-bit working precision) for $k=K,\dots,K+29$ ($K=70$), the
worst such ratio $r$ is checked to be $<1$, and the tail beyond $K$ is bounded by the standard geometric
sum $|a_K|\,r/(1-r)$. This reproduces, and is the honest version of, the ``genuinely convergent /
super-geometric decay'' language in the note -- the earlier script's stopping rule (two consecutive
terms below \texttt{TOL}) is here replaced by an explicit checked ratio bound, so the resulting tail pad
is a proved upper bound, not an empirical stopping heuristic.

\paragraph{The rigorous certificate, stated precisely (post-review, Reviewer AT: two gaps in this
paragraph fixed, no computation changed).} For each of $j=4,5$: $B$ is holomorphic and
zero-free-verified-so-far on the disk $D=\{|t-t_0|\le\rho\}$ except possibly at the winding-detected
zero; every one of $4096$ points on $\partial D$ satisfies $|B(t_i)|\ge\min_\partial|B|>0$ (Arb ball,
rigorous); and by the Cauchy estimate on the concentric circle of radius $2\rho$, $|B'(t)|\le M$ for all
$t\in D$ with $M$ as tabulated. Since $M\cdot(\text{chord}) < \min_\partial|B|$ in both cases, for any
two adjacent sample points $t_i,t_{i+1}$ the straight segment between them lies in $D$ (convex) and the
Mean Value Theorem bound $|B(t_i)-B(t_{i+1})|\le M\cdot|t_i-t_{i+1}|<|B(t_i)|$ holds; this places $B$
restricted to the segment inside the open disk of radius $|B(t_i)|$ centered at $B(t_i)$, a disk that
excludes $0$. \textbf{This closes the segment, not yet the boundary arc between $t_i$ and $t_{i+1}$;
the bridge is the following elementary fact, stated explicitly:} for any point $s$ on the minor arc
between $t_i,t_{i+1}$, the chord length $|s-t_i|=2\rho\sin(\delta/2)$ (where $\delta\in[0,\pi/2048]$ is
the arc-angle from $t_i$ to $s$) is monotonically increasing in $\delta$, hence bounded above by the
full inter-sample chord $2\rho\sin(\pi/4096)$ used in $M\cdot(\text{chord})<\min_\partial|B|$; so the
same MVT bound $|B(s)-B(t_i)|\le M\cdot|s-t_i|\le M\cdot(\text{chord})<\min_\partial|B|\le|B(t_i)|$
applies verbatim to every point on the arc, not just the sampled endpoints. Hence $B$ has no zero
anywhere on $\partial D$ (not merely at the $4096$ sample points) and $\arg B$ cannot turn by $\ge\pi$
between consecutive samples (the bound above shows the angular sweep along each arc is in fact
$\le2\arcsin(M\cdot\text{chord}/\min_\partial|B|)\approx8^\circ$, far below $\pi$, so the certificate
has real slack, not a knife-edge margin), so the true continuous winding number of $B$ around
$\partial D$ equals the verified discrete winding number, $1$, exactly. By the argument principle $B$
has exactly one zero (counted with multiplicity) inside each disk $D$.

\emph{On the tail bound (Reviewer AT, item 5):} the geometric tail-ratio bound $r_B$ was checked over
a window $k=K,\ldots,K+23$ ($K=70$) via rigorous Arb upper bounds on consecutive term ratios, then
extrapolated to all $k\ge K$; the extrapolation (ratio sequence non-increasing beyond the checked
window) was not itself proved in-code, though it is analytically defensible (the dominant factor
$|q|^{2k+1}$ strictly decreases and the Pochhammer-denominator correction is squeezed toward $1$). This
is downgraded from ``validated'' to ``checked over a window; not proved to extrapolate.'' It does not
threaten either PASS verdict: the realized tail pads are $10$--$13$ orders of magnitude smaller than
the certified margins, so even a grossly weaker tail bound would not flip either result.

Reviewer~AQ identified is closed for
