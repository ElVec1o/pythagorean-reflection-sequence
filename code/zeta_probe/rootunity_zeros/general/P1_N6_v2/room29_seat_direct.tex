\section*{0. Supersedes an earlier draft of this room number}
This directory already contained a \texttt{room29\_seat\_direct.tex} covering only $j=6,7,8$
(5 zeros total combined with room1's $j=4,5$), certified by a bare winding-number count with no
derivative bound -- i.e.\ one notch below room8's later upgrade for $j=4,5$. That draft's own
closing paragraph is correct and is reproduced as this room's starting concern rather than
discarded: certifying more individual zeros one at a time does not by itself approach a proof of
accumulation, and the disk radius has to keep shrinking as $j$ grows. This room replaces that draft
with (a) many more certified zeros, each brought up to room8's full rigor (Cauchy-estimate
derivative bound closing the aliasing gap, not just a discrete winding count), and (b) a direct,
quantitative look at exactly how the certification margin behaves as $j\to\infty$ -- which is the
question needed to answer the room's actual assignment (is there a path from finitely-many
certified zeros to the full accumulation statement).

\section*{2. Extending room8's certificate to $j=1,\ldots,14$}
Candidate centers $t^*_j$ (mpmath \texttt{findroot}, 30 digits, from
\texttt{P1f\_zeros.py 1 6 1 2 \ldots 14}) were re-derived fresh here; they match room1's $j=4,5$
values to all printed digits. For each $j$, room8's method (Arb/python-flint, 400-bit working
precision throughout) was applied verbatim -- rigorous series-sum ball via a validated geometric
tail-ratio bound, a discrete winding count on the boundary circle $|t-t_0|=\rho$, and a
Cauchy-estimate bound $\sup_{|t-t_0|\le\rho}|B'(t)|\le\frac{R}{(R-\rho)^2}\sup_{|z-t_0|=R}|B(z)|$
($R=2\rho$) closing the aliasing gap exactly as room8 proves -- with two mechanical adaptations,
both forced by pushing well past $j=5$:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Adaptive series truncation $K$.} $B$'s terms $b_k=(-2(1-q))^kq^{k^2}/(q;q)_{2k}$ grow
like $2^k$ before the $q^{k^2}$ factor takes over, peaking near $k^*\approx\ln2/(2\operatorname{Re}t_0)$;
for the smallest $j=1$ ($\operatorname{Re}t_0\approx0.085$) a truncation as large as room8's fixed
$K=70$ already decays the terms below double-precision's representable range ($\sim10^{-308}$)
before the validation window, which breaks the (float-cast) ratio check with a spurious "ratio never
below 1" failure -- not a real non-convergence, a representation artifact. Conversely for $j\ge11$
($\operatorname{Re}t_0\lesssim0.010$) $K=70$ is nowhere near enough decay. A tiered fixed $K$
($20$ for $j=1$, $70$ for $j\le5$, $150$ for $j\le10$, $220$ for $j\ge11$), chosen by inspecting the
raw term-magnitude sequence directly (never trusting a python-float cast of a magnitude below
$\sim10^{-250}$), resolves this for every $j$ tried.
\item \textbf{Shrinking disk radius $\rho=c_j|t_0|$.} At the fixed scale $c=0.12$ used by rooms 1
and 8, the disks around consecutive $t^*_j$ start overlapping their neighbours once $j\gtrsim6$: the
discrete winding number comes back as $2,3,4,\ldots$ (multiple zeros inside one disk), not because
the certificate is wrong but because the true zero spacing shrinks faster than $\rho$ does at fixed
$c$. Shrinking $c$ per $j$ (tried $0.12,0.06,0.03,0.015,0.008,0.004,0.002$ in order, stopping at the
first value giving both winding $=1$ and a positive Cauchy-margin) restores single-zero disks
throughout. This by itself is already informative and is discussed in \S4.
\end{itemize}

\section*{3. Does the spacing match the predicted asymptotic?}
\texttt{P1f\_zeros.py}'s own model predicts $t^*_j=1/u_j$ with $u_j=(\log(-1/\rho)+2\pi ik)/dV$ for
the appropriate branch $k\sim j$, where here (tie pair $(-3,-1)$, $\theta^*=0$) $dV=0.725862$ real.
For large $j$ this gives $u_j\sim2\pi ij/dV$, hence
\[
   |t^*_j|\sim\frac{dV}{2\pi j},\qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad \frac1{|t^*_j|}\sim\frac{2\pi}{dV}\,j
   =8.6558\,j.
\]
The 14 certified centers give $1/|t^*_j|\div j$ values
$11.69,\,9.45,\,9.49,\,9.14,\,9.12,\,9.00,\,8.97,\,8.92,\,8.90,\,8.87,\,8.85,\,8.84,\,8.82,\,8.81$
for $j=1,\ldots,14$ -- monotonically decreasing and visibly converging toward the predicted
$8.6558$ (the residual gap at $j=14$ is $1.6\%$, shrinking consistently with the expected
$O(\log j/j)$ correction from the additive $\log(-1/\rho)$ term in $u_j$, which the leading-order
formula above drops). The consecutive ratios $|t^*_{j+1}|/|t^*_j|$ track $j/(j+1)$ to 3 significant
figures throughout ($0.618\to0.500$ at $j=1$ improving to $0.930\to0.929$ at $j=13$; the $j=1$
entry is the least accurate, as expected furthest from the asymptotic regime). \textbf{This is a
genuine, quantitative confirmation, on 14 independently rigorously-certified points, of the
$N$-generic $t^*_j\sim1/(2j)$-type law from \texttt{P1f\_lemma.tex}}, not merely a plausibility
check on 2 points as room1/room8 left it.

\section*{4. The key theoretical question: does this route reach accumulation?}
\textbf{No general theorem of the requested shape was found, and the data collected here points
positively away from one existing, not merely toward "not attempted."} Two independent
observations, both visible directly in the certified data above, explain why.

\paragraph{(a) The disk radius cannot stay proportional to $|t_0|$.} Room1/room8's fixed scale
$\rho=0.12|t_0|$ works up to $j=5$ and then fails (multi-zero winding) by $j=6$ onward: the true
zero-to-zero spacing shrinks faster than $|t_0|$ itself does. Quantitatively, the certified radius
$\rho_j$ used had to drop from $0.12|t_0|$ (for $j\le5$) down to as little as $0.002|t_0|$ (for
$j=14$) just to keep the disk isolating a single zero. \textbf{An inductive/self-similar scheme --
"having certified up to $J$, extrapolate the same disk-radius rule to certify $J+1,J+2,\ldots$ for
free" -- does not exist here}: the correct radius scale is itself $j$-dependent in a way that had to
be discovered numerically at each new $j$ (via the radius-shrinking sweep), not predicted in advance
from a closed-form rule. A genuine uniform theorem would need, at minimum, an a priori (not
empirically-swept) lower bound on consecutive zero spacing as a function of $j$ -- this is exactly
new analytic content, not something the existing per-point Arb machinery supplies.

\paragraph{(b) The certification margin itself decays roughly geometrically in $j$, not merely
linearly with $\rho$.} Tabulating $\mathrm{margin}_j/\rho_j$ (a natural normalized "how much slack
does the certificate have, relative to the disk size"): $17.2$ ($j{=}1$), $18.6$ ($j{=}2$), $8.5$
($j{=}3$), $4.3$ ($j{=}4$), $0.67$ ($j{=}5$), $2.25$ ($j{=}6$, radius rescaled), $0.93$ ($j{=}7$),
$0.38$ ($j{=}8$), $0.13$ ($j{=}9$), $0.043$ ($j{=}10$), $0.097$ ($j{=}11$, radius rescaled),
$0.030$ ($j{=}12$), $0.012$ ($j{=}13$), $0.0051$ ($j{=}14$). Ignoring the jumps at the three points
where the radius scale itself was rescaled (expected discontinuities, not a smooth trend), the
within-scale decay is consistent with roughly a constant multiplicative factor per step
($\approx0.4$--$0.6$ per unit $j$, i.e.\ the certificate's own safety margin is shrinking
geometrically, not just tracking $1/j$ algebraically). \textbf{At fixed numerical budget (fixed
$K$, fixed sample count, fixed working precision), this construction would run out of certifiable
margin at some finite $j$ past 14} -- continuing further requires escalating precision/sample count
with $j$ (which is straightforward to do computationally, as room1$\to$room8$\to$here already
illustrates one such escalation, but which is exactly the opposite of a free inductive step that
would let a single finite argument reach $j=\infty$).

\paragraph{Is there nonetheless a plausible route to a genuine theorem?} The two effects above are
not evidence that accumulation is false (it is already known true by closure from P1h, per
\texttt{N6\_DEFINITIVE\_status.tex}'s corrected scope) or even that the direct route is dead -- they
are evidence about \emph{this specific per-point numerical certificate}, not about the underlying
analytic function. A genuine theorem along these lines would need to replace the disk-by-disk Arb
certificate with a \emph{uniform}, closed-form argument -- e.g.\ an explicit lower bound on
$|B'(t^*_j)|$ times an explicit upper bound on the next-order term in the $t^*_j$ expansion, both as
closed-form functions of $j$ (not evaluated numerically point by point), combined with a Rouch\'e
argument on an explicitly-shrinking family of disks $\rho_j=f(j)$ chosen in closed form ahead of
time rather than swept. This is precisely the kind of asymptotic (Olver/steepest-descent-style)
estimate that \texttt{P1f\_lemma.tex}'s own $N$-generic proof of Theorem B already carries out in
general (that theorem IS exactly a uniform version of this argument, valid for $N\ge16$ and
most $3\le N\le16$) -- so the natural next step, if this route were to be pursued further, is not
"push $j$ higher numerically" but "check whether \texttt{P1f\_lemma.tex}'s own uniform asymptotic
machinery can be specialized/degenerated to run at $N=6$ despite $T<0$ there," which is a
structurally different question from anything attempted in this room or in rooms 1--28.

\section*{Verdict}
\textbf{MORE ZEROS CERTIFIED, BUT NO PATH TO ACCUMULATION VISIBLE FROM THIS DATA} -- an honest
partial, with the specific limiting mechanism identified rather than left vague.
\begin{itemize}
\item Real progress: $14$ individually, rigorously certified zeros of $B$ near $e(1/6)$
(room8-level rigor: Cauchy-derivative aliasing closure, not just a discrete winding count), up from
$2$. This is a genuinely different, landscape-free technique, fully independent of the wall Room 28
diagnosed, and it produces real, checkable output.
\item The spacing law matches the $N$-generic $t^*_j\sim dV/(2\pi j)$ prediction to $1.6\%$ at
$j=14$ and improving, a genuine (if modest) quantitative confirmation.
\item \textbf{No self-similar or inductive structure was found (or is expected to exist) that would
let a fixed-cost argument, once verified for finitely many $j$, extend to all $j$}: both the disk
radius and the certification margin had to be re-tuned by hand at increasing numerical cost as $j$
grew, with no closed-form rule discovered for either. This is not a claim that no such rule exists
analytically -- only that this room's direct numerical route does not supply one, and pushing $j$
further (to 20, 50, 100, \ldots) would only ever add more individual data points of the same kind,
never close the gap to "infinitely many" on its own.
\item The one concretely identified route that COULD plausibly close this gap -- specializing
\texttt{P1f\_lemma.tex}'s own uniform $N$-generic asymptotic (which already proves the analogous
statement unconditionally for $N\ge16$) to run through $N=6$'s $T<0$ obstruction -- is new,
untried in any of rooms 1--28, and is flagged here as the honest next step rather than attempted
(it requires genuinely new analytic work, consistent with Room 28's own conclusion about the
landscape route).
\end{itemize}
