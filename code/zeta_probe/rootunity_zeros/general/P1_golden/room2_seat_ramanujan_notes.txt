1. THE nan NEAR ell~0.035, 0.012 -- DIAGNOSIS: TWO DIFFERENT BUGS, NOT ONE
=====================================================================

1a. The 1/12 family (x=1/12=0.08333 > x1_good=0.08043, i.e. genuinely just inside the good arc,
    ell~0.035-0.051): THIS NAN IS A GENUINE, FIXABLE PRECISION BUG IN THE SCRIPT -- NOT a
    formulation/branch-cut issue, and NOT fixed by the script's own escalation to 3200 bits.

    Root cause, isolated by hand: P1e_logderiv.py line 15,
        PI = arb.pi(); I = acb(0, 1)
    is executed ONCE at module-import time (right after `ctx.prec = 200` on line 14) and is never
    recomputed. `data()`'s precision-escalation loop (lines 17-22: pr in 200,400,800,1600,3200) raises
    ctx.prec for every OTHER arb/acb operation, but every call to e(y) = (2*PI*I*y).exp() still uses the
    stale PI/I baked in at 200-bit (~60 decimal digit) accuracy. This caps the effective precision of the
    single most basic ingredient -- the root-of-unity phases z0, ET[...] -- at ~60 digits FOREVER, no matter
    how high ctx.prec is later pushed. I confirmed this is exactly what happens: raising ctx.prec by hand to
    6400 and 12800 bits (without also refreshing PI/I) reproduces the identical nan, byte for byte
    (same |Z_q|=0, Lam=nan) -- matching the note's own observation that 3200 bits didn't help, and now
    showing WHY: the escalation loop was not actually escalating the quantity that matters.

    Traced further at p/q=17/203 (q~200 point of the 1/12,theta=+1 family): the series in data1() sums
    M~1145 terms; u^n (tracked via repeated ball multiplication `un = un*u`) has a TRUE magnitude (checked
    independently in plain double-precision complex arithmetic, which agrees with |u|=0.9613 raised to the
    1145th power) that decays smoothly and monotonically to ~2.4e-20 by n=1145 -- there is no real blow-up.
    But in the Arb ball computation, u's OWN radius is only ~3.5e-61 (i.e. ~200-bit-precision-sized, matching
    the stale-PI diagnosis), and this fixed absolute radius does not shrink as ctx.prec is raised for later
    operations. Printing un's ball at n=600..710 shows the radius overtaking the (correctly decaying) midpoint
    exactly around n~650-700: abs_lower(un) hits exactly 0 there, and abs_upper(un) then runs away to
    1e19..1e22 by n~1145 -- not because u^n is large, but because once the enclosure straddles 0 the reported
    upper bound is dominated by accumulated radius, not signal. This propagates into Ph[k], then b[k]=exp(Ph[k])
    (genuinely infinite for the affected residues), then f0 = sum g_k b_k has an enclosure so wide that
    F = |f0|.abs_lower() = 0 exactly, and Lam = env/F divides by exact 0 -> nan. That is the entire mechanism.

    FIX VERIFIED: recomputing `PI = arb.pi(); I = acb(0,1)` at the START of every precision-escalation
    iteration (i.e. inside data()'s pr-loop, right after `ctx.prec = pr`) completely removes the nan.
    With the fix, p/q=17/203 resolves cleanly at pr=400 (not even needing 3200):
        |Z_q|=[10.205435 +/- 2.26e-7]  Lam=[5.65466 +/- 3.66e-6]  D1=[0.124535 +/- 4.30e-7]  D2=[0.0069676 +/- 4.85e-9]
    and the whole 1/12 family (both theta=+1 and theta=-1) resolves cleanly out to q~3000-3011 with D1,D2
    small and, if anything, slowly SHRINKING with q (see Sec.2 table). This is a one-line, low-risk fix to
    the actual repository script (move the PI/I assignment inside the retry loop) -- flagged for a separate
    engineering task, NOT applied to the repo here per the read-only instruction for this room.

1b. The 1/13 family (x=1/13=0.076923 < x1_good=0.080432, theta=+1, "fails already at q=51/64"):
    THIS NAN IS GENUINE AND STRUCTURAL, A DIFFERENT ISSUE FROM 1a -- the whole family sits on the wrong
    side of the good-arc edge, not merely close to it.

    Checked directly (plain complex arithmetic, no Arb needed): for every q tried in this family
    (p/q = 5/64, 16/207, 77/1000, 231/3002) one gets |c0| = |-2(1-z0)| < 1, i.e. l = log|c0| < 0
    (-0.0285, -0.0390, -0.0427, -0.0434 respectively) -- NEGATIVE, not "close to 0 from above". The
    entire premise of the u-formula (and the whole finite-formula framework, which is built on |u0|<1,
    equivalently |c0|>1) is violated for this family at every q, not just asymptotically. Concretely this
    makes X=c0^q -> 0 as q grows instead of -> large, so B=2+X -> 2 and 4/B^2 -> 1, and the branch
    computation `(1 - 4/(B*B)).sqrt()` sits right at (and, with the sign flip from l<0, effectively past)
    a genuine square-root branch point -- this is exactly the kind of input for which the formula was never
    claimed to converge. The concrete exception raised (`ValueError: cannot convert float NaN to integer`,
    from Mn = int(45/(-float(ua.log().mid())))+5 when |u|>=1 so log(ua)>=0) is a downstream symptom, not the
    cause. This is a genuine domain/formulation boundary (the family 1/13 itself starts on the wrong side of
    x1_good), not a precision-loss artifact, and not fixable by more bits. It is, however, a minor robustness
    gap that the script does not check the |c0|>1 precondition and fail with a clear message instead of a
    bare stack trace -- flagged, not fixed, per read-only instructions.

Bottom line on the nan: the note's single "precision-collapse phenomenon" is actually two unrelated things
that happened to coincide near the same numerical value of ell: an ordinary (and fixable) precision bug in
the escalation loop for seeds still inside the good arc (1/12), and a genuine, expected breakdown for a seed
family that lies entirely outside the good arc (1/13). Neither is evidence about Z_q's true behaviour; the
1/12 case, once fixed, in fact adds MORE data points (q up to ~3000-4000) supporting boundedness of D1,D2,
not against it.

3. VERDICT
=====================================================================
D1/D2 LOOK UNIFORMLY BOUNDED ACROSS THE WINDOW.

Specifically:
  - No evidence of D1 or D2 growth was found anywhere across ell in (0.012, 0.48) once the precision bug
    (Sec. 1a) is corrected -- including at the seeds nearest the golden threshold and nearest the good-arc
    edge that are actually inside the domain (1/12, ell~0.035-0.051).
  - NAN IS A GENUINE (FIXABLE) PRECISION ISSUE for the 1/12 family: a stale module-level constant (PI, I in
    P1e_logderiv.py, computed once at ctx.prec=200 and never refreshed by the precision-escalation retry
    loop) silently caps the effective precision of every root-of-unity evaluation at ~60 decimal digits
    regardless of how high ctx.prec is later raised -- explaining why the note's own escalation to 3200 bits,
    and my further escalation to 6400/12800 bits, did not help. One-line fix (recompute PI, I inside the
    retry loop) verified to resolve it cleanly.
  - Separately, NAN IS A DEEPER (BUT EXPECTED, NOT A BUG) FORMULATION-BOUNDARY ISSUE for the 1/13 family:
    that entire family sits at x=1/13=0.076923, which is already less than the good-arc edge x1_good=0.08043,
    so |c0|<1 (l<0) at every q in the family -- the finite-formula framework's own precondition is violated
    from the start, not approached asymptotically. This is a genuine domain limitation (and a minor
    robustness gap: the script should detect |c0|<=1 and fail with a clear message rather than a bare
    ValueError), not evidence of anything about Z_q, and not fixable by precision.
  - The one family showing strong decay of |Z_q| itself (1/11, theta=+1, new seed here) still has bounded,
    unremarkable D1/D2 throughout the decay -- consistent with the note's own point (b) that genuine mass
    loss in some residue classes is compatible with, and does not require abandoning, boundedness of the
    moment ratios a second-order Lemma R would need.

No counterexample to the moment hypothesis was found in this session's survey.
